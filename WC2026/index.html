Kolja Knauer (University of Barcelona): Endomorphisms of sparse graphs

Abstract: An endomorphism of a graph is a homomorphism from the graph to itself. Since endomorphisms are closed under composition, they form a monoid. It has been known since the 1960s that every finite monoid is isomorphic to the endomorphism monoid of some graph. This naturally led to the question of how restrictive a graph class G can be while still realizing every monoid (from a given class) as the endomorphism monoid of a graph in G.

Particular attention has been devoted to sparse graph classes, such as planar graphs, minor-closed classes, and bounded-degree graphs. In this talk, I will survey some of the developments in this area and present two recent results that answer questions posed by Babai and Pultr in the 1980s:

1. Every commutative idempotent monoid (equivalently, every finite lattice) is isomorphic to the endomorphism monoid of a subcubic graph.

2. Every monoid is isomorphic to the endomorphism monoid of a regular graph.

Joint work with Gil Puig i Surroca.

Introduction: Kolja Knauer is currently Professor at the University of Barcelona and visiting professor at Hebei Normal University. His research lies in combinatorics, particularly oriented matroids, algebraic graph theory, and their connections with geometry and group theory. Before joining the University of Barcelona, he held positions in Berlin, Montpellier, and Aix-Marseille. He is also Editor-in-Chief of the Annals of Combinatorics.


Jack Koolen (University of Science and Technology of China): On co-edge-regular graphs with 4 distinct eigenvalues

Abstract: In this talk I will discuss some constructions of co-edge-regular graphs with four distinct eigenvalues. The main goal is to show that they do not behave like strongly-regular graph.

This talk is based on joint work with Hong-Jun Ge and Edwin van Dam.

Introduction: Jack Koolen is currently a full professor at the University of Science and Technology of China. He received his Ph.D. from Eindhoven University of Technology in 1994 under supervision of Prof Andries Brouwer. After a few post doc positions in for example Oxford University and Kyushu University, he became an associate professor in POSTECH in Pohang Korea. Since 2013 he is a full professor at the University of Science and Technology of China. His main interest is in algebraic graph theory especially distance-regular graphs. Recently he also got interested in graphs with fixed smallest eigenvalues. He has co-authored a book on phylogenetic combinatorics.

He is currently a managing editor of Graphs and Combinatorics and an associate editor of Designs Codes and Cryptography. He received the excellent supervisor award of the Chinese Academy of Sciences.


Amanda Montejano (National Autonomous University of Mexico): How should a discrete analogue of the Brunn–Minkowski inequality look like?

Abstract: The Brunn–Minkowski inequality is a fundamental result in convex geometry. In the continuous setting, Bonnesen’s refinement incorporates (d−1)-dimensional projections, yielding sharper bounds that reflect geometric structure. A discrete counterpart is known only in dimension two, due to Grynkiewicz and Serra. In this paper, we explore extensions of this result to higher dimensions by introducing a general framework to derive discrete Brunn–Minkowski-type inequalities.

Introduction: Amanda Montejano is a Full Professor at the National Autonomous University of Mexico (UNAM). She received her Ph.D. in Mathematics from the Polytechnic University of Catalonia, in Barcelona, Spain. Her research interests lie primarily in combinatorics, with a particular emphasis on Ramsey theory, extremal graph theory, and additive combinatorics. Alongside her research, she is passionate about making mathematics accessible to wider audiences. She is the author of the popular mathematics book in México called "Matemáticas de Colores" ("Colorful Mathematics").


Paolo Santonastaso (University of Naples Federico II): Algebraic aspects of rank-metric and sum-rank-metric codes

Abstract: In this talk, we present a unified viewpoint on rank-metric and sum-rank-metric codes based on skew polynomial rings. Within this framework, codes can be represented as subsets of suitable quotients of skew polynomial rings. This provides a natural description of several known constructions and offers new insights into their algebraic structure. We also discuss how this perspective leads to new constructions of optimal codes in both the rank and sum-rank metrics.

Introduction: Paolo Santonastaso obtained his Master’s degree in Mathematics from the University of Campania "Luigi Vanvitelli" in Italy, where he also received his Ph.D. in Mathematics, Physics and Engineering Applications in 2023 under the supervision of Olga Polverino and Ferdinando Zullo. He subsequently held postdoctoral positions at the University of Campania "Luigi Vanvitelli" and the Polytechnic University of Bari. He is currently an Assistant Professor at the University of Naples Federico II. His research interests include coding theory, finite geometry, noncommutative algebra, and code-based cryptography.


Leonid Shalaginov (Chelyabinsk State University): Divisible design graphs with extremal parameters

Abstract: Divisible design graphs form an important class of highly regular graphs with close connections to combinatorial design theory and finite geometry. In this talk, we survey the current state of the classification of divisible design graphs, with particular emphasis on the cases (\lambda_1 = k-1) and (\lambda_2 = k-2), for which complete classifications are known. We discuss the main ideas underlying these classification results. We then present recent progress on the classification of divisible design graphs with (\lambda_1 = k-3) or (\lambda_2 = k-3).

Introduction: Leonid Shalaginov is a Professor in the Department of Mathematics at Chelyabinsk State University, Russia. His research interests include algebraic graph theory, combinatorial designs and highly regular graphs. His recent work focuses on the classification and structural properties of divisible design graphs and related combinatorial structures.


Ferdinando Zullo (University of Campania "Luigi Vanvitelli"): From arcs to MRD codes: Finite geometry meets the rank metric

Abstract: In this talk I will discuss some constructions of co-edge-regular graphs with four distinct eigenvalues. The main goal is to show that they do not behave like strongly-regular graph. This talk is based on joint work with Hong-Jun Ge and Edwin van Dam.

Introduction: Classical coding theory and finite geometry are connected by a rich dictionary: linear codes correspond to configurations of points in projective spaces, while MDS codes correspond to arcs. We explore how this dictionary extends to rank-metric codes. In this setting, q-systems, linear sets, and scattered subspaces provide geometric models for MRD codes. This viewpoint translates questions about the construction, existence, equivalence, and classification of rank-metric codes into problems in finite geometry. We illustrate how results and techniques flow in both directions, producing new codes, new geometric structures, and new perspectives on longstanding problems
